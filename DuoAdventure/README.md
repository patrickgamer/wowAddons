# DuoAdventure 0.3.0

Quest planning from each class's first playable level, solo or with a party. Switch between **adventure/immersion** and **grinding/speed levelling** without changing native dialogue, quest acceptance, turn-ins or rewards. It works independently; DuoSocial adds optional local session-plan integration.

## Implemented and everyday use

- `/duoadventure` opens/closes the searchable quest planner outside combat. Search title, quest-log zone/header or quest ID. **Adventure** / `/duoadventure mode adventure` preserves exposed native log order, shows objective text and story/campaign labels, and encourages reading full narrative in native quest details. `immersion` is an alias.
- **Speed** / `/duoadventure mode speed` sorts your **accepted** quests by manually ranked pins, completion, current-zone header, zone grouping and available objective distance within a zone, with native order as a tie breaker. `grinding` is an alias. This is a transparent planning heuristic, not an optimal path, XP/hour model, combat recommendation or database of undiscovered quests. It does not account for travel terrain, portals, prerequisites or reward value.
- `/duoadventure pin ID 1` assigns rank 1 (1–999, lower first). `/duoadventure pin ID` toggles a pin. `/duoadventure focus ID` chooses your current plan; `unfocus` clears it. Focus is manual and does not supertrack/move/target anything.
- `/duoadventure note ID private text` stores a private quest note; `unnote ID` removes it. `/duoadventure open ID` opens supported native quest details or explains the fallback. **Native quest log** opens the client log when its entry point is available. Read/share/accept/turn in manually using native UI.
- **Social plan** or `/duoadventure social` copies the selected focus/mode and a catch-up reminder into **your local** DuoSocial activity field. It never sends the text/notes to another player and works only while DuoSocial is enabled. Quest planning remains available without it.
- **Progress sharing on/off** or `/duoadventure share on|off` gives or withdraws this party's consent for addon progress reports; **Request progress** or `/duoadventure share` asks opted-in party installations for fresh reports. The original `/duoadventure sync on|off` and `/duoadventure sync` still work exactly as before.
- `/duoadventure native` prints WoW's own Party Sync state, read-only. It works in combat and changes nothing.

## Progress sharing is not WoW Party Sync

DuoAdventure **progress sharing** is this addon's own exchange of quest-progress reports over the party addon channel. WoW **Party Sync** is a separate native game session that can put quests on hold when the synced group does not meet their requirements; held quests cannot be completed until you leave Party Sync. The panel shows the two on separate lines:

- `Progress sharing (DuoAdventure addon reports, not WoW Party Sync): ON | OFF …` is the addon's consent and report status.
- `WoW Party Sync (native, read-only): …` is what WoW reports through `C_QuestSession`: **ACTIVE for you**, **a session exists in your group** (WoW does not report you as joined), **a start/stop request is pending** (answer it only in WoW's own dialog), **not active, as reported by WoW**, or **unknown**. Unknown means WoW did not return a usable answer (missing API, inaccessible or non-boolean value, or partial data); DuoAdventure never treats that as "not active".

When WoW reports Party Sync active for you, DuoAdventure prints one notice in your chat frame (again only after the session ends and a new one starts, or after a reload) and the join prompt repeats it. The notice explains the possible holds and the manual exit: **Native quest log**, then **Stop Party Sync** at the bottom of the Map & Quest Log, then WoW's confirmation. If you only see its active status, the party member with that control must stop it. Leaving removes session holds, not other requirements, and temporary replay quests disappear. Turning progress sharing on or off never changes Party Sync, and leaving Party Sync never changes progress sharing.

DuoAdventure never starts, joins, answers, stops or supertracks a native Party Sync session, never answers invitation, leave-party, convert-to-raid or ready-check confirmations, and does not hook or click native dialogs. Opening quest details or the native quest log only displays them; Party Sync starts only from WoW's own button and confirmation. DuoSocial's **Invite partner** remains an explicit click; if Party Sync is already running, WoW shows its own confirmation, which you answer yourself. `tools/check-repository.py` rejects those native calls in every addon, and `tools/verify-native.lua` runs every DuoAdventure and DuoSocial entry point with Party Sync first off and then already active. Showing the notice does not lift holds, prove who started a session, or show which requirement a held quest failed.

## Sharing and catch-up advice

Each quest shows confirmed native party overlap. For connected members not reported on the same quest, it explains that they could have already completed it, lack prerequisites, be in another phase or have incomplete roster data. `IsPushableQuest` determines whether the local quest permits native sharing; it does not prove the other player can accept it.

When sharing is available: open that quest's native details, discuss whether they need it, use the **native Share button**, and wait for acceptance. If sharing is unavailable: catch up at the quest giver/prerequisite chain as shown by native tools. Before your own completed-quest turn-in, the panel reminds you to confirm whether the party needs the quest or more objective credit. Same quest ID does not prove equal progress or shared eligibility. These are out-of-combat quest-log planning suggestions, not restricted combat-data prompts. No quest is shared or turned in automatically.

## Progress sharing between party installations

On joining an ordinary party, each installation shows a **DuoAdventure - share quest progress** prompt with a **Share quest progress automatically** checkbox and **Apply toggle / Keep off** buttons. The prompt states that these are addon reports, not WoW Party Sync, and adds the native notice when WoW reports a session or pending request. No quest data is published until you apply an enabled toggle. Its previous setting is preselected, but each new party session (including login/reload while grouped) needs consent again. Closing the prompt leaves sync off. If you join during combat, the prompt waits until combat ends. Consent covers the party including members who join later; changing membership clears old snapshots and triggers a fresh exchange without prompting existing opted-in members again. Leaving a party resets session consent.

Once enabled, sharing is automatic: quest acceptance/removal/turn-in, completion and incremental objective changes trigger a debounced broadcast to party installations. A **30-second refresh** keeps quiet peers current and recovers from temporary chat restrictions. There is no need to click Request progress after each kill or quest change. Rapid changes are coalesced for about 1–3 seconds; a single paced outbox sends the latest snapshot without flooding. During a large transfer, later progress is coalesced until the current transfer drains.

**Progress sharing on/off** or `/duoadventure share on|off` (also `sync on|off`) changes consent explicitly; **Request progress** / `/duoadventure share` (also `sync`) remains an optional manual refresh. Turning sharing off stops automatic publishing/replies and clears local peer data immediately; WoW Party Sync, if any, is unchanged. Party recipients also need to opt in to process snapshots. Receivers update their plugin panels silently, without ordinary chat or per-kill popups.

The versioned `DuoAdventureV2` protocol uses Blizzard's **PARTY addon channel**, not ordinary chat. It shares exposed quest IDs, ready-to-turn-in flags, finished/total objective counts and the first six accessible numeric objective progress pairs per quest (for example `3/10`, rounded to two decimal places). Other objectives still contribute to the completion summary. It sends **no private notes, objective text, chat text, guild data, bindings, identity configuration or other-addon settings**. Existing party character names label native senders. Party-channel packets are visible to party addons listening on this prefix, so this is party communication rather than an encrypted exchange. All participating clients need v0.2 or later (protocol V2; 0.3.0 keeps V2 unchanged); V1 clients cannot exchange progress with V2.

Received progress is labelled **addon-reported snapshot**, with time since receipt. It is never proof of eligibility, phases or future state. Snapshots expire 120 seconds after receipt, clear on combat/group changes/leaving world, and are memory-only. No data can mean declined/missing addon, different protocol, combat/chat restrictions, offline member or packet loss. Progress summaries cannot substitute for inspecting individual objectives together.

Ordinary parties only; raids/instance-chat-specific distribution are unsupported. Combat/chat lockdown prevents send/receive: updates during a kill fight are **deferred**, then the current public quest state is rebuilt after combat. Chat-only restrictions recover on the next periodic refresh. Queued packets are cancelled on group/combat changes. Packets are bounded (240 bytes, 200 quests, six numeric progress pairs), sent at four packets/second through a bounded queue. Current-party senders/channel, nonces, numeric bounds and complete assembly are validated; new pushes require your session opt-in, while request replies require a pending request. Incomplete transfers can be replaced by newer snapshots; wrong-channel/off-party traffic is ignored. Peers remain untrusted reporters. Real-client communication is still unverified.

## Limitations

Only quest-log entries exposed by the client are used. Expand collapsed headers in native UI if entries are missing. Hidden/world/task/event quests or uncached data may not be represented. Distance may be unavailable or only a straight-line objective estimate, and may change after travel: click Refresh. Current-zone header matching is textual and can miss expansion/campaign headers. Nothing changes native watched quests, dialogue or quest-log ordering. Planning pauses in combat. Offline party data is unknown; no hidden progress is inferred. No route database, auto-loot/interaction, reward choice, accept/turn-in or visible chat sender exists.

## Installation on Windows and macOS

Keep `DuoAdventure/DuoAdventure.toc`, `Common.lua`, `Planner.lua`, `Native.lua`, `Sync.lua` and `Main.lua` together directly under the repository. The existing Windows whole-folder AddOns junction from `<RETAIL_ADDONS_PATH>` to `<REPOSITORY_PATH>` exposes it automatically. On macOS follow the [root symlink instructions](../README.md#installation-and-first-use) with actual local repository/Retail paths. Do not link WTF. Restart WoW to discover the addon; enable it and use `/reload` for subsequent edits. DuoSocial is optional and declared only for load order. Both party members need DuoAdventure and progress-sharing opt-in for direct progress snapshots; the native overlap view works without the other person's addon.

## SavedVariables and reset

`DuoAdventureDB`, schema 1, is account-wide in normal `WTF/Account/<account>/SavedVariables/DuoAdventure.lua`. It stores mode, pins, private notes, focus and the progress-sharing toggle preference (key `sync`, unchanged in 0.3.0, so no migration runs). Saved toggle state preselects the next party prompt; it does not grant session consent automatically. Settings are shared across account characters by quest ID; defaults merge non-destructively. Peer data, consent, timers and queues are memory-only. Exit WoW, back up and remove only `DuoAdventure.lua` and `.lua.bak` for a reset; this never changes actual quests/native tracking. Back up WTF separately and keep notes out of Git.
